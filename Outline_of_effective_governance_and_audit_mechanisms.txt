To break the insulation in this system, governance and audit frameworks must shift accountability from transaction-point execution to long-term lifecycle performance. Structural interventions must target the financial, operational, and informational layers of the market to force decision-makers to absorb downstream consequences.

1. Financial Re-Coupling (Aligning Economic Incentives)

• Deferred Transaction Compensation: Retain a percentage of broker commissions and purchaser bonuses in an escrow account. Release funds only after the product meets verified operational milestones over a 3-to-5-year period.
• Malus and Clawback Clauses: Enforce contractual mandates to reclaim past payouts if a product is later found to have systematically underrepresented defects at the time of sale.
• Shared Liability Pools: Require independent brokers to hold performance bonds or insurance. This ties their financial survival directly to the long-term accuracy of their product disclosures.

2. Operational & Audit Re-Coupling (Exposing True Performance)

• Post-Acquisition Lookback Audits: Mandate independent audits 12 to 24 months post-transaction. These audits must compare actual operational utility against the initial marketing representations.
• Immutable Decision Logging: Record all procurement justifications, product claims, and risk waivers on a tamper-proof ledger. This prevents decision-makers from erasing their paper trail when moving to new roles or organizations.
• Defect-Weighted Performance Metrics: Evaluate procurement officers on the net-present value and operational uptime of assets over time, rather than the volume or speed of transactions completed.

3. Informational Re-Coupling (Restoring the Commons)

• Decentralized Reputation Protocols: Establish a shared, anonymized registry where purchasing organizations log product failures and misrepresentations. This dilutes information asymmetry and penalizes dishonest brokers.
• Blinded Third-Party Verification: Require products to be certified by independent, technically competent auditors who are compensated via a blind pool. This prevents brokers from "ratings shopping" or bribing validators.
• Whistleblower Bounties for Market Disclosures: Incentivize internal engineers or analysts to flag known, unrepresented product defects before transactions close.
